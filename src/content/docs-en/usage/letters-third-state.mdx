If they're away, out of contact for a long time, or you just want everything to go quiet for a while — none of these states means "it's over".

The reason people open Mirrai is often precisely "they're not around". Far away, out of touch for ages, freshly split — these three used to be the same thing in a product, and all three read as "it's over". Not anymore. Here is what each one is and how to use it.

## The five forms, at a glance

- **Letters** — write it down and keep it, or hand it to them
- **Save it for when they're back** — hold onto what you want to say
- **Temporarily out of touch** — not a breakup; just far away
- **Take a pause** — not a goodbye; just a pause
- **What time is it where they are** — time zone, last contact, will they speak first

One by one, below.

## Letters: some things can't be said into a chat box

A letter comes in two forms:

- **Just write it down**: kept with you, never answered. It's yours.
- **Hand it to them**: a letter you hand over gets a reply in **about a day**. Real people don't reply instantly — that part is on purpose.

**What you should see**: letters you only wrote sit quietly in the list; handed-over letters show as delivered, and the reply arrives about a day later.

## Save it for when they're back

Write down what you want to say and take it out when you're back in touch. It's just a list — nothing goes out on its own.

**What you should see**: those words appear under the "say it when they're back" list, ready to use or delete any time.

## Temporarily out of touch

**This is not a breakup.** While it's on:

- Anniversary reminders stop;
- Their tone shifts toward an **epistolary** style — no pressing, no assuming the other side has seen anything;
- The lobby spells out why they've gone quiet. Silence without an explanation looks like a malfunction.

**What you should see**: they say little and write like letters; the lobby reads "temporarily out of touch", so you know the quiet has a reason.

## Take a pause

Too noisy, or too tired. Let it go quiet for a while:

- Reminders stop, they don't speak first, and they don't bring up **goodbye**;
- But chat continues whenever you want, and the memories are all still there.

This is not a breakup, and it is not a **goodbye** — just a pause. Right after a split, what many people need is exactly this: neither a farewell nor more conversation — just stop for a bit.

**What you should see**: they no longer show up on their own and never mention graduation; when you start a chat, they reply as usual.

## What time is it where they are

Three small things that give "not around" a definite shape.

### Time zone

The presence speaks by the clock where they are. When it's the middle of the night on their side, the interface notes "it's the middle of the night where they are".

**What you should see**: when they say "it's late now", it's counted on their clock, not yours.

### Last contact

An honest number — as many days as it's been since you last spoke.

**What you should see**: the day count matches when you last actually spoke.

### Will they speak first

The default is **never**. Turn it on and they'll send a message when you've gone a long time without talking; how often they reach out is yours to decide.

**What you should see**: with it off, they never pop up out of nowhere; with it on, a message from them arrives after a long silence.

## What's next
