When you've said what you wanted to say, or the system tells you you're eligible to graduate — here is how graduation works, how to choose among the three modes, and what happens after the goodbye.

Mirrai's goal is not to keep you here; it is to help you finish the **goodbye**.

## How intimacy grows

Intimacy grows naturally with conversation: **acquaintance → familiar → close → confidant → soulmate**.

When the system detects your emotional state settling — positive feelings taking up more room, regrets and "what ifs" thinning out — it will tell you you're eligible to **graduate**. That is a prompt, not an execution: whether and when to graduate is always your call.

## The three modes of the graduation mechanism

| Mode | Behavior |
|------|----------|
| **System suggestion** | prompts you once when conditions are met |
| **Manual only** | the system never brings it up; you click it yourself when you're ready to **say goodbye** |
| **Off** | the mechanism stays out of the way entirely |

The global default is changed in Settings, and each presence can be set separately — the final say is yours.

- In **system suggestion** mode, when conditions are met you'll see one prompt; accepting or refusing are both fine;
- In **manual only** mode, you pick the moment of **goodbye** entirely yourself, and the system stays quiet;
- In **off** mode, the mechanism disappears from your use of the app.

The suggestion is made **only once**: afterwards it enters a cool-down period, and only after that can it come up again. A refusal is not a closed door — you can change your mind any time.

**What you should see**: in whichever mode you chose, the system behaves as described — silent when it should be silent, and speaking only once when it should speak.

## What happens at graduation

### A farewell letter

The AI writes a **farewell letter** in **their voice**. It is not a summary in the system's words — it reads like the kind of thing they would write to you.

**What you should see**: a farewell letter that reads like them, kept for good.

### Dormancy

The presence goes into **dormancy**: no more anniversary reminders, no more showing up in "who to talk to today", no more messages.

**What you should see**: the presence quieting down in the lobby, but still there.

### You can wake them any time

Dormancy is not deletion — you can **wake them any time**, and waking loses nothing. The farewell letter stays, and so do the memories.

**What you should see**: whenever you want to speak again, you can wake it, and the farewell letter is where it was.

## It also keeps an eye on the pacing

The emotional safety layer watches usage intensity: when the last 7 days run more than half again as busy as the 7 before, and the rise holds for 3 days straight, it will nudge you back toward the **goodbye** suggestion. When a single session runs too long, or a day gets too chatty, a line of care shows up in the replies.

It doesn't interrupt you — it just puts "maybe take a breath" in front of you.

## Graduation also has an exit

What usually goes unsaid is not their last line — it's **yours**: the things you only said here and never dared say to the person themselves.

- **The exit**: those words are collected and put in front of you, and you decide whether to say them. **The system never sends on your behalf**: the presence cannot pass messages to people on the outside, and cannot claim it carried any line of yours to them.
- **Write it to yourself**: if that person is no longer here, this letter has a definite destination, instead of lying in a list forever.
- **Let it go**: that too is an ending — recorded, not deleted.

**What you should see**: those words are gathered and set in front of you; the system performs no "delivered for you" action — whether to say them, whether to send them, stays in your hands.

## Take your own things with you

All data can be exported as JSON or Markdown, including memories, diaries, the **farewell letter**, and the event stream. After graduation, these things are still yours.

## When you don't want to graduate

Right after a split, or when it's too noisy or too tired, use **take a pause** (see [When they're not around](/en/docs/usage/letters-third-state)): no proactivity, no talk of **goodbye**, but chat continues and the memories stay.

A pause is not a **goodbye**. Whenever you're ready, graduation is still there.
